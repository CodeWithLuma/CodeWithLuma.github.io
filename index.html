03 — FRAUDPYTHON · SCIKIT-LEARN · SQL

How do you spot fraud in 0.17% of transactions?

Only 492 out of 284,807 transactions were fraud. That is the whole problem — a model that guesses "not fraud" every time scores 99.83% and catches nothing.

492frauds in 284,807 transactions
0.172%fraud rate — about 1 in 579
0.838average precision, random forest (published run)

Data: Worldline / Université Libre de Bruxelles credit card dataset, via Kaggle — European cardholders, September 2013, two days. Features V1–V28 are anonymised, so no real customer behaviour can be read from them.

Pythonscikit-learnSQL

What I'd recommend: judge fraud models on precision and recall, never on accuracy. On data this imbalanced, accuracy is a trap.

05 — PRICINGPYTHON · SQL

What sets the price of a night in New York?

I live next to New York City. I wanted to know what sets the price of a night here — the borough, the room, or something else.

$149median night, Manhattan
$65median night, the Bronx
$160median night, entire home

Data: NYC Airbnb Open Data, via Kaggle — 48,895 listings, 2019 snapshot. Reviews per month is a demand proxy, not bookings, and the data has no seasonality.

PythonpandasSQL

What I'd recommend: price by borough and room type, not by a citywide average. Manhattan costs 2.3× the Bronx, and an entire home costs 2.3× a private room — that gap is the story.
